The pressure dropped as the center passed to the east

His readings are shifted down by 0.11 inHg. That adds 0.03 for his 24 ft elevation and subtracts 0.14 because the gauge read high. The 0.14 comes from his 4–6 PM readings, which ran above the nearby stations by a steady 0.13–0.15. Destin Airport sat almost on the storm's path and bottomed out at 29.00 inHg right at landfall.

The flat stretch from 6:30 to 7:45 PM, followed by a sudden drop at 8:16, may mean the needle stuck while the pressure was falling fast. Aneroid dials often lag like this. Tapping the glass before each reading frees the needle. The barometer was indoors, so the bump at 7:24 could also come from wind changing the air pressure inside the house, which can move a reading by a millibar or more. Gusts were near their peak then, and the wind direction was shifting as the center passed.

He was outside the eye, on its west side

The track runs north across the coast about 20 miles east of Navarre. The dashed circle has a radius of 17 miles (15 nautical miles), which is roughly the NHC's estimate of where the strongest winds were around landfall. Navarre sits just outside it. If the eye had passed overhead, his reading would have bottomed out near Destin's and stayed flat for a while. Instead it dipped once and recovered within an hour.

The shoreline is a rough sketch through the beach towns, not a survey line. The NHC rounds center positions to 0.1° (about 7 mi), so distances are good to a few miles.

How to read this

  • His lowest pressure came at 8:16 PM, about 15 minutes before the NHC's landfall time. That fits a center passing to his east and moving north at about 19 mph.
  • His corrected low of about 990 mb was 8 mb higher than Destin's 982 mb and 19 mb higher than the storm's 971 mb center. Pressure rises quickly with distance from the eye.
  • His reading between 8:16 and 9:04 PM is missing, so the true low may have been a little lower than 29.35 on the gauge.
  • Navarre was on the west side of the storm, which is usually the weaker side. Onshore winds and the highest storm surge are usually east of where the center crosses the coast.

Sources: NHC best track AL092026 (preliminary) and hourly position updates and advisories (Discussion 14); airport observations from the Iowa Environmental Mesonet ASOS archive. The NHC's final storm report will come out months from now and may revise these numbers.
